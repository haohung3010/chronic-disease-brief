# Chronic disease in British Columbia: where the caseload is growing

*A one-page brief for a health-system manager. Data: Public Health Agency of Canada, Canadian Chronic Disease
Surveillance System, fiscal year 2023-24 against 2013-14. Both sexes.*

## The answer

**Hypertension and diabetes add the most patients, and they do so even where the rate is flat or falling.** British
Columbia has about 240,000 more people living with hypertension than ten years earlier, and about 164,000 more living
with diabetes. Planning that follows the age-standardized rate alone will underestimate demand, because the rate
removes the ageing and growth that fill clinics.

| condition | people living with it | added in 10 years | prevalence, age-standardized | vs Canada | new-case rate, 10-yr change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Hypertension | 1,174,755 | +239,735 (+26%) | 24.2% | -0.4 pts | +13% |
| Diabetes | 562,280 | +163,940 (+41%) | 9.3% | -0.1 pts | +7% |
| Asthma | 573,705 | +127,300 (+29%) | 9.8% | -1.9 pts | -18% |
| COPD | 346,025 | +70,405 (+26%) | 9.6% | +0.1 pts | -46% |
| Dementia | 71,885 | +20,275 (+39%) | 5.6% | -0.2 pts | +3% |

## What it means

* **Size services on counts, compare on rates.** Hypertension's age-standardized prevalence fell from 25.4% to 24.2%
  over the decade while the number of people with it rose by a quarter.
* **Diabetes is the one to watch.** It grew fastest in people (+41%), its new-case rate is up 7%, and the new-case rate
  in BC, Alberta and Ontario rose after 2020 (partly a rebound from a 2020 dip). BC sits 7th of 13 provinces and territories, close to the Canadian rate.
* **BC is not an outlier.** On every condition here BC is within 0.4 points of Canada except asthma, where it is
  1.9 points below.

## What to check before acting

* The COPD new-case rate halving (1,048 to 565 per 100,000) is large enough to be a coding or data change rather than
  a change in disease. Confirm with PHAC's notes before using it.
* Counts of people overlap across conditions: someone with diabetes and hypertension is in both rows. Do not add the
  column.
* The data see people who used insured care. Undiagnosed disease is not counted.
